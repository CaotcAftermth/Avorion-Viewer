# Weapon Coverage

Open **Properties > Turrets > Weapon Coverage**, enable **Show Weapon Coverage**, and choose **Directional Slices** or **Spherical Heatmap**. Attach turrets from the Asset Library first. This is geometric visibility analysis, not a weapon damage or combat simulation.

## Operation

- **Top/Bottom**, **Left/Right**, and **Front/Back** align the existing camera to the horizontal, longitudinal, or transverse plane. **Slice position** moves that plane along its perpendicular native axis. Every target is recomputed at the new position; the display is not a translated arc drawing. **Reset slice** returns to the ship bounding center.
- **Directional slices** answer two questions per bearing in the slice plane. *Arcs at infinity:* each turret aims along the bearing at a target infinitely far away, so only traversal limits and structure decide it. The band just outside the analysis radius colors each bearing by its count, and one thin ring per turret (numbered like the mount markers) shows where it is clear (its color), in arc but blocked (amber) or unresolved (purple). Arcs depend on direction only, so slice position does not change them. *Dead-zone field:* inside the radius, finite targets along each bearing are colored by count, and white lines mark where the clear count crosses 1 (nothing can hit inside it) and 2. Arc edges are bisected to 0.05° and field edges to 1% of the radius; both use the full engine for every probe. **Slice style > Legacy field view** restores the earlier blended per-turret fields.
- **Dead-zone analysis radius** (Slice extent in the legacy view) is the circular slice radius. **Sphere radius** is distance from the ship bounding center, constrained to enclose the model. The default is 1.5 times a conservative bounding radius. These distances are not weapon range.
- **Coverage Display** chooses clear, potential, obstructed turret counts, blind-spot classifications, or **Change vs Baseline** (see Statistics and comparison). Potential always equals clear plus obstructed for resolved contributors. A turret with any eligible clear muzzle counts once as clear. Inspection also reports partial obstruction between that turret's muzzles.
- **Turret Selection** includes all enabled turrets, the selected turret(s), or a named group. Selection is a display filter over one analysis of every enabled turret: changing it never re-runs the worker, and a turret outside the filter still blocks the others' rays. Select mounted turrets normally with inspection off, or use the Outliner. **Selected turret · user-defined aiming** controls inclusion, group, color, fixed/coaxial assumption and spread, azimuth arc and elevation limits. The azimuth arc is an **Azimuth center** plus an **Azimuth half-width** (180° means full rotation), so rear-facing and wrap-around arcs such as 150°..−150° are expressed directly. Older mounts that stored minimum/maximum azimuth are converted on read. These settings use mount undo history and ship-session persistence.
- **Inspect coverage points** enables hover details and click-to-pin a sampled cell. Details contain ship-relative direction, target coordinates, counts, classification, contributor names, and blocking block IDs. Pinned inspection can select the contributing turrets or open a vertical slice through the point. The inspected position is the cell's analyzed center, not an interpolated cursor position.
- **Frame coverage** fits the analysis extent in the active viewport. Existing orbit and zoom controls work normally. **Show Ship** changes display only, keeping collision analysis intact. **Show Grid** strengthens the globe's subtle latitude/longitude references or adds circular slice guides.
- **Individual turret colors** (legacy field view only), enabled by default, draws each turret's continuous sampled field with boundary-only outlines. Overlapping fields blend; they do not encode a numerical count. Turn this off or use the spherical heatmap for counts. **Show only overlapping clear coverage** filters to samples with at least two clear turrets. **Show Structural Obstructions** retains fully blocked samples in aggregate displays; blocking always applies to the clear-field calculation.
- **Low / Medium / High** directional slices use 180 / 360 / 720 arc bearings and 72 / 144 / 288 field bearings with 8 / 12 / 16 distances each, plus adaptive bearings (1 / 2 / 3 levels) where neighboring field profiles disagree. Work grows with the number of edges, not the slice area; a safety cap of 200,000 evaluations is reported if reached. Fixed weapons seed their boresight bearing so cones narrower than the coarse step are found. Arcs appear first; the field streams in after. The legacy field view uses 576 / 2,304 / 6,912 planar targets in concentric sectors. Spheres use 80 / 320 / 1,280 spherical targets. Medium and High first display a Low preview. Change quality to investigate narrow gaps. Legacy slice boundaries are refined breadth-first: every boundary triangle is subdivided one level before any goes deeper, ranked by how many probes disagree, so an exhausted evaluation budget leaves approximation spread evenly instead of concentrated in one region. A warning reports when the budget was reached.
- Analysis uses a dark backdrop and draws the ship over the field for a readable silhouette. Viewport grids and post-processing are suppressed while a result is displayed; normal rendering resumes when coverage is disabled. Explicitly enabled sockets and placement dragging remain available, and Select Sockets takes priority over coverage inspection. Image exports retain their existing rendering path.
- **Statistics and comparison** lists solid-angle-weighted percentages for the globe (bearing-angle-weighted arcs at infinity for directional slices, area-weighted for the legacy view). Directional slices also list the percent of 360° covered by 1+ and 2+ turrets, the longest blind arc, each turret's clear and blocked arc width, and the mean and maximum dead-zone depth. All views list covered, overlapping (2+ clear), blind, structurally blocked and unresolved, overall and per direction (Front/Rear/Top/Bottom/Right/Left; slices omit the plane-normal pair). **Save as comparison baseline** stores the current spherical result; after editing the layout, **Change vs Baseline** colors each direction by its change in clear turrets and the panel lists per-direction differences. A baseline only compares against a sphere with the same radius and quality. **Export coverage CSV/JSON** downloads one row per analyzed target with weights, counts, classification and contributing turret IDs (JSON format `avorion-weapon-coverage`, version 1).
- **GPU hull rays (experimental)**, in Analysis settings, casts hull rays in a WebGL2 fragment shader over the same BVH. Aiming, turret parts and any ray the shader could not finish stay on the CPU path. It needs float render targets; when unavailable the analysis falls back to CPU with a warning.

## Counts and color

The globe uses a continuous count gradient: 0 black, 1 blue, 2 cyan, 3 green, 4 yellow, and 5+ red/orange at the default absolute scale. Adjust the upper bound for larger turret counts, or explicitly enable automatic scaling. The viewport legend runs from more weapons to fewer. A blue/orange alternative palette is available.

Blind Spots uses dark gray for mechanically uncovered, pink for fully structurally obstructed, amber for partially obstructed, and green for covered. Invalid inside-geometry cells are omitted from slices and gray on the globe. Nonconvergent custom-axis targeting is purple/unresolved. Globe counts interpolate across shared geodesic vertices on a separately tessellated spherical surface. Slice field contours interpolate sampled membership. These are presentation approximations: they neither create new engine results nor guarantee exact obstruction boundaries. Inspection always reports an original analyzed target and its integer counts. Purple is used only for the unresolved classification: a turret that has a resolved path through any muzzle is never reported as unresolved, and an unresolved turret no longer hides clear coverage from other turrets.

## Geometry and aiming

The engine uses native ship coordinates: +Y up, +X right, +Z front. The existing camera converts native coordinates to viewport coordinates. It does not rotate collision geometry separately from turrets. Each target is an actual finite-distance position, and each turret calculates a direction from its own aimed muzzle to that target.

The existing attachment, mount twist, uniform source-size scaling, yaw pivot, pitch pivot, and elevation sign drive aiming. Standard mechanisms (vertical yaw axis, horizontal pitch axis) are aimed in closed form: each muzzle's yaw and pitch equations have up to two solutions each, which are verified against the full pose. Unusual custom axes use a damped numerical solve seeded from the closed-form guess and the previous solution for that muzzle, trying every seed before reporting the target unresolved. Native designs point +Z; generic designs use the existing −Z convention. Native muzzle points are preferred. Assigned muzzle blocks or barrel bounds supply explicitly labeled estimated tips when native data are unavailable. Missing sockets or muzzle geometry produce warnings rather than fabricated positions.

Imported XML provides pivots, muzzle positions, size and a coaxial flag, but no verified traversal limits. Default azimuth is an explicitly user-defined ±180° assumption; elevation uses design limits (normally −15°/+90°). Users can narrow these per mounted turret. Native coaxial mounts default to the current fixed pose, also explicitly labeled an assumption. A fixed weapon covers a cone around its barrel direction: the larger of the configurable spread half-angle (default 1°, up to 45°) and the angle the target cell subtends from the muzzle, so fixed guns register on any sampled cell they actually point into. Inspection reports the off-axis angle. Each muzzle is solved independently: a clear count means at least one usable firing path, not simultaneous convergence of every barrel. The application currently supports rigid attachment frames with uniform turret scaling; it does not supply arbitrary nonuniform parent transforms.

Collision queries reuse the viewer's actual block-surface triangles, including wedges and corners, in a bounding-volume hierarchy. Boxes only prune intersection candidates. Rays are two-sided and bounded by the target distance, so geometry beyond the target does not obstruct it. A scale-aware origin tolerance suppresses numerical self-hits without excluding nearby hull blocks. Native muzzle markers can sit inside their own barrel blocks (as in Craft 2). Those containing barrel solids are excluded only from that muzzle's own firing ray. Hull blocks, base/body geometry, separate barrel pieces, and other turrets still obstruct, including geometry close to or overlapping the muzzle. The firing turret's remaining parts are tested in their solved pose; other turrets remain at their displayed poses. Block and turret IDs accompany the nearest hit. BVH traversal is nearest-first and skips nodes entered beyond the current nearest hit; other turrets are pre-filtered by their bounds. Point-inside checks reject targets within included closed block geometry.

Hidden blocks/turrets, transparent blocks, glass/holograms, and solid decorative blocks are excluded by default. Separate inclusion controls expose those assumptions. Explicit `coverageBlocking` block overrides are supported internally. Unknown block types retain the existing placeholder geometry and generate an approximation warning. With no hull geometry, analysis reports an error rather than asserting clear lines of sight.

## Architecture and performance

- `src/turrets/coverage-engine.js`: constraint adapter, closed-form and numerical targeting, fixed cones, per-turret caches, counts, validity, obstruction rules. Engines split into `plan` (inside check and aiming) and `resolve` (rays) so hull rays can be batched.
- `src/turrets/coverage-gpu.js`: flattened BVH layout and WebGL2 hull-ray caster.
- `src/turrets/coverage-stats.js`: weighted statistics, baselines, differences and CSV/JSON export.
- `src/turrets/fire-coverage.js`: reused block BVH/muzzle utilities, extended with bounded hit records and point-inside queries. Older sweep helpers remain for regression compatibility; the main UI no longer uses their directional-sector renderer.
- `src/turrets/coverage-polar.js`: directional slices: arc bearings at infinity, radial dead-zone profiles, 1D bisection, adaptive bearings, angular summaries.
- `src/turrets/coverage-sampling.js`: plane/geodesic target layouts with solid-angle/area weights and footprints, shared options, colors, classification, display filters, analysis keys.
- `src/turrets/coverage.worker.js`: background preparation, progressive Low-to-requested-quality passes, cooperative cancellation.
- `src/turrets/coverage-state.js`: scene invalidation, persistent worker, request generation checks, current-result cache.
- `src/turrets/coverage-surface.js`: smooth spherical display tessellation and continuous per-turret slice contours, with original sample mappings.
- `src/turrets/coverage-render.js`: batched Three.js heatmap/line geometry in the existing renderer, sample picking, legends and inspection text.
- `src/turrets/coverage-ui.js`: Properties controls, editable assumptions, groups and coordinated views. `editors.js`, `app.js`, `viewport.js`, and `game-renderer.js` connect the feature to existing editors and rendering.

The globe uses geodesic triangle cells with no longitude seam or repeated pole samples. Geodesic cells are not equal-area, so statistics weight each cell by its exact solid angle (Van Oosterom–Strackee) rather than counting cells. Aggregate count colors come from the shared engine, not transparent-cone overlap. Only the globe's outward faces render, avoiding front/back color accumulation. Low-level geometry is batched rather than drawn separately for each turret/target pair.

The worker retains ship collision geometry across turret and sampling changes. Model/geometry revisions, mounts, analysis settings and inclusion rules invalidate the appropriate data. Editing one turret rebuilds only that turret's posed geometry and BVH; unchanged turrets are reused. Selection and group filters are display-only. Camera, opacity, count colors, grid, inspection and display-only ship visibility reuse calculations. Generation IDs discard stale replies; workers are terminated when analysis is disabled or all turrets are removed. Geometry/materials are disposed when replaced.

## Validation

`tests/weapon-coverage.test.mjs` covers clear/blocked/outside-arc targets, count invariants, overlapping turrets, partial obstruction, mounting surfaces, nearby armor, finite targeting with separated turrets, aimed pivot/muzzle offsets, coordinate transforms, custom axes, BVH invalidation, inclusion rules, inside-hull targets, sphere radius, enable/fixed constraints and missing geometry.

`tests/weapon-coverage.check.mjs` exercises the actual main-app UI, globe rendering and inspection, slice movement, cached presentation changes, stale-request rejection, obstacle visibility invalidation, and worker shutdown. Its synthetic workload contains 5,020 hull blocks and 20 four-block demo turrets. One local headless Edge run completed the progressive medium globe in approximately 646 ms while the main-thread timer continued running. This is an analysis measurement, not a GPU frame-rate guarantee or a bound for arbitrary imported ships.

The presentation update passed 234 unit tests and a production build. Surface regressions cover circular slice extents, plane offsets and seams, shared fan boundaries, all overlapping contributors, spherical tessellation, continuous colors and exact sample mappings. The muzzle-origin regression retains the actual Craft 2 block/muzzle arrangement, nearby hull and separate-geometry obstruction checks, and transformed/scaled mounts. Earlier browser checks covered independent Properties scrolling, six standard camera directions, placement/size/history/persistence, pose/Outliner controls, and image/blueprint export parity; those older export checks are not new measurements of this presentation change.

The subsequent armored-placement regression passed 237 unit tests and the production build. Coverage no longer suppresses socket markers or intercepts Select Sockets clicks. Armored cube and edge bases are tested across all 24 orientations, including attachment and restored socket IDs. GX4 was verified in the main app: its seven armored bases and two armored edge bases expose sockets, and a native size-10 turret was placed on its thin top plate with Fit Base choosing size 1.5.

The improvement pass (closed-form aiming, fixed cones, display-only filters, incremental rebuild, breadth-first refinement, GPU rays, center/half-width arcs, statistics/comparison/export and persisted settings) passed 242 of 243 unit tests; the remaining failure, `tests/turret-lab.test.mjs`, needs the local prototypes folder and was not run in the test environment. `tests/weapon-coverage-improvements.test.mjs` covers the new behavior. In headless Chromium with SwiftShader the 5,020-block slice check fell from about 80 s to about 31–37 s and the medium globe took about 1.1 s. `tests/weapon-coverage-gpu.check.mjs` matched the CPU on all 20,000 test rays and every sphere cell, but the GPU globe took about 12 s against 0.35 s on CPU under software rendering. `tests/weapon-coverage-tools.check.mjs` checks statistics, baseline, export and reload persistence in the main app.

## Limitations

Directional slice arcs assume an infinitely distant target; near targets are in the dead-zone field, which evaluates finite positions only along its sampled bearings. Fixed weapons appear in the field only where their cone passes through a probe; their arcs are exact. Slice baselines compare statistics only, as before.

Results are approximate geometric visibility under stated constraints, not verified Avorion targeting. Sampling can miss thin obstacles and narrow clear corridors. Numerical targeting may not find every solution for unusual custom pivot mechanisms; unresolved custom-axis targets are labeled. Rotation paths are not checked for turret/hull collisions, projectiles do not curve, and other turrets do not dynamically re-aim during obstruction checks.

Coverage display settings (visualization, metric, quality, slice, filters) and the saved baseline persist with the ship session; per-turret constraint/group settings persist with mounts. The analysis overlay is currently viewport-only and is excluded from image/blueprint export rendering. Existing export behavior is preserved.

GPU hull rays are experimental. They use 32-bit floats, with coordinates recentered on the ship, and can disagree with the CPU on grazing hits; rays exceeding the traversal limits are repeated on the CPU. On a software GPU (SwiftShader) they are much slower than the CPU path; speed on real hardware has not been measured. Leave the option off unless it measurably helps on your machine.
